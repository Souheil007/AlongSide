## 1. Pediatric Nutrition Science & Milestones (0–12 Months)

### 1.1 The Feeding Continuum: From Liquid Nutrition to Table Foods
For the first 6 months of life, human breast milk or iron-fortified infant formula provides 100% of the caloric, fluid, and nutritional requirements for growth.

```
NUTRITIONAL PHASES IN INFANCY:
┌────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┐
│ 0–6 Months: Milk Exclusivity│ 6–8 Months: Complementary  │ 9–12 Months: Finger Foods  │
├────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┤
│ • Human milk or formula    │ • Introduction of solids   │ • Three meals + 2 snacks   │
│ • Vitamin D (400 IU/day)   │ • Iron & zinc dense foods  │ • Pincer grasp feeding     │

│ • 24–32 oz/day volume      │ • Purees or Baby-Led (BLW) │ • Water in open cup (4-8oz)│
└────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1.2 Supplementation Standards (AAP)
- **Vitamin D (400 IU/day):** All breastfed infants and formula-fed infants consuming <32 oz/day must receive 400 IU liquid Vitamin D daily starting in the first few days of life to prevent rickets and support immune maturation.
- **Iron:** Full-term infants have maternal iron stores that deplete around **4–6 months**. Breastfed infants require iron supplementation (1 mg/kg/day) starting at 4 months until iron-rich complementary foods (meat purees, iron-fortified cereals) are consumed consistently.

### 1.3 Introducing Complementary Solids: The 4 Readiness Signs (at ~6 Months)
1. **Head & Neck Control:** Infant can sit upright with minimal support and hold head steady.
2. **Loss of Tongue-Extrusive Reflex:** Baby stops automatically pushing food out of their mouth with their tongue.
3. **Hand-to-Mouth Dexterity:** Baby actively reaches for food, grasps objects, and brings them to the mouth.
4. **Active Interest in Food:** Leans forward, opens mouth when food approaches, tracks caregivers' utensils.

### 1.4 Baby-Led Weaning (BLW) vs. Purees & Allergen Introduction
- **The LEAP Trial Paradigm:** Delaying allergenic foods (peanuts, eggs, dairy, tree nuts, fish, shellfish, soy, wheat, sesame) increases the risk of food allergies. Early introduction between 4–6 months for high-risk infants (with eczema or egg allergy) and by 6 months for general infants reduces peanut allergy by over **80%**.
- **Choking vs. Gagging Differentiation:**
  - *Gagging:* Loud, coughing, red face, tongue thrust forward. This is a normal, life-saving protective reflex. Do NOT panic or thrust fingers into the mouth.
  - *Choking:* Silent, blue/pale lips, wide-eyed terror, inability to cry or vocalize. Requires immediate pediatric back blows and chest thrusts.
- **Strict Prohibitions:**
  - **Zero Honey under 12 months:** Risk of fatal infant botulism (*Clostridium botulinum*).
  - **Zero Cow's Milk as primary beverage under 12 months:** Causes intestinal micro-bleeding and lacks necessary iron.
  - **Zero Whole Nuts, Whole Grapes, Popcorn, Raw Carrots, Hot Dogs:** Severe choking hazards.

### 2.1 The Infant Fever Protocol
- **Under 3 Months (<90 Days Old):** A rectal temperature of **≥ 100.4°F (38.0°C)** is an immediate pediatric medical emergency. Young infants cannot localize infections; bacteremia, meningitis, or UTI must be ruled out immediately in an Emergency Department. Never administer infant Tylenol to mask a fever in an infant <3 months without direct physician orders.
- **3–6 Months:** Temperature ≥ 101°F warrants clinic evaluation. Acetaminophen (Tylenol) dosed by weight (not age) may be used.
- **6–12 Months:** Temperature ≥ 102.5°F or fever lasting >72 hours warrants clinical visit. Ibuprofen (Motrin) is approved only starting at 6 months of age.

## 3. AAP Safe Sleep Environment (SIDS / SUID Reduction)

1. **Back to Sleep (Supine Position):** Every sleep (naps and night) must be on the back until age 1. Once an infant can roll independently from back to tummy AND tummy to back, they may remain in the position they choose.
2. **Firm, Flat Sleep Surface:** Crib, bassinet, or play yard meeting CPSC standards with a tight-fitting sheet. Never let an infant sleep on an adult bed, sofa, armchair, pillow, or inclined sleeper.
3. **Bare Sleep Space (Zero Fluff):** Zero blankets, zero pillows, zero crib bumpers, zero stuffed toys, zero positioners. Use a fitted wearable blanket (sleep sack) for warmth.
4. **Room-Sharing Without Bed-Sharing:** Infant should sleep in the parents' room, close to the parents' bed, on a separate firm surface for at least the first **6 months**. This reduces the risk of SIDS by up to 50%.
5. **Avoid Overheating & Smoke Exposure:** Keep room temperature between 68°F–72°F (20°C–22.2°C). Zero nicotine/vape smoke exposure.

## 4. Daily Routine Templates by Age Tranche

### 4.1 Tranche A: 0–3 Months (The Fourth Trimester Rhythm — E.A.S.Y.)
*Rhythm, not rigid clock schedule (Cycle repeats every 2.5–3 hours):*
- **E (Eat):** Nursing or bottle feeding upon waking (2.5–4 oz).
- **A (Activity):** 10–20 minutes of diaper change, upright eye contact, 2–3 minutes tummy time.
- **S (Sleep):** Swaddle, white noise, put down drowsy at 45–60 minute wake window mark.
- **Y (You):** Caregiver takes 1 hour to rest, hydrate, or nap.

### 4.2 Tranche B: 7–9 Months (2-Nap Biological Schedule)
- **7:00 AM:** Wake, morning breastmilk or formula feed (6–8 oz).
- **8:00 AM:** Breakfast solids (iron-fortified oatmeal with mashed berries, scrambled egg strip).
- **9:30 AM – 11:00 AM:** **Nap 1 (1.5 hours).**
- **11:00 AM:** Milk feed (6 oz) + outdoor floor play / stroller walk.
- **12:30 PM:** Lunch solids (avocado slices, steamed broccoli floret, shredded chicken).
- **2:30 PM – 4:00 PM:** **Nap 2 (1.5 hours).**
- **4:00 PM:** Milk feed (6 oz) + floor exploration, music, reading board books.
- **5:45 PM:** Dinner solids with family (mashed sweet potato, soft lentils).
- **6:30 PM:** Bedtime wind-down routine (warm bath, infant massage, lullaby, sleep sack).
- **7:15 PM:** Bedtime milk top-off and placed in crib asleep/drowsy for night.
