## 1. AAP Clinical Policy on Media Exposure (0–18/24 Months)

The official American Academy of Pediatrics clinical guidance for infants under 18–24 months is unambiguous:

> **AAP Clinical Standard:**  
> **Avoid the use of screen media other than video chatting.**  
> For infants under 18 months, evidence demonstrates that digital media exposure provides zero cognitive benefit and impairs language acquisition, sleep consolidation, and joint attention.

### 1.1 The Lone Exception: Interactive Video Chatting
Interactive video chatting (e.g., FaceTime with deployed parents or distant grandparents) is medically permitted because:
1. It involves **live social reciprocity**: the person on the screen responds in real time to the infant's facial expressions and vocalizations.
2. An adult caregiver is present to scaffold and interpret the interaction ("Look, there's Grandma smiling at you!").

### 2.1 The 2D-to-3D "Video Deficit Effect"
Research led by Dr. Georgene Troseth and Dr. Rachel Barr demonstrates that infants under 2 years experience a profound cognitive hurdle known as the **Video Deficit Effect**:
- If an adult hides a toy in a room, an infant who watches the hiding in person finds the toy 85% of the time.
- If the infant watches the identical event broadcast live on a high-definition television monitor, finding success drops to below 25%.
- The immature prefrontal and parietal cortices cannot translate two-dimensional pixel arrays into three-dimensional physical reality without hands-on tactile manipulation.

### 2.2 Auditory Deprivation via "Background TV"
A study by Dr. Dimitri Christakis published in *Pediatrics* revealed that when a television is playing in the background (even if the infant is not directly watching it):
- Every hour of audible background TV is associated with a **reduction of 770 words** spoken by caregivers to the infant.
- Vocalizations by the infant drop by **40%**.
- Conversational turn-taking collapses by **82%**, directly delaying speech onset.

## 3. "Technoference" & Dr. Edward Tronick's Still Face Experiment

When caregivers look down at smartphones during feeding, diapering, or floor play, they unintentionally subject the infant to an acute micro-version of the **Still Face Experiment**:

```
TRONICK'S STILL FACE SEQUENCE:
[ Interactive Smiling / Eye Contact ] → [ Caregiver Face Goes Blank / Glued to Phone ] →
[ Infant Smiles & Points to Re-engage ] → [ Screeching / Distress Flail ] → [ Autonomic Shutdown / Aversion ]
```

- When an infant seeks eye contact and encounters a motionless, blank parental face focused on a smartphone screen, the infant's heart rate spikes and cortisol levels elevate.
- If technoference is chronic, the infant internalizes that their emotional bids for connection are ineffective, fostering anxious or avoidant attachment patterns.

## 4. The Infant Digital Sanctuary Pact (Clauses for AlongSide Pacts)

Parents in Tier 0A should implement these three non-negotiable clauses in their AlongSide Family Agreement:

1. **The Screen-Free Nursery & Feeding Sanctuary:** Zero phones or tablets active during nursing or bottle feeding. Feeding is reserved for skin-to-skin touch, maternal eye contact, and vocal attunement.
2. **Background TV Prohibition:** Televisions remain powered off when an infant is awake in the living space. Music or podcasts may be played through smart speakers without accompanying visual screens.
3. **The Phone-Free Floor Play Anchor:** Caregivers designate at least two 20-minute windows daily where smartphones are placed inside a physical docking box in another room while engaging in uninterrupted floor play.
