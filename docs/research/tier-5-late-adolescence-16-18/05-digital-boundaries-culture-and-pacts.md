## 1. The Transition to Digital Sovereignty

Between ages 16 and 18, parental digital management must execute its final, deliberate evolution:
- **In Early Adolescence (Ages 13–15):** AlongSide used **Pact-as-Code** (OS-level profiles, neutral 8:30/9:00 PM locks) to provide the external prefrontal braking system that the teen’s brain lacked.
- **In Late Adolescence (Ages 16–18+):** The goal is **Digital Sovereignty**: handing over the steering wheel so the emerging adult learns to build their own internal digital boundaries before arriving at an unstructured college dorm or workplace.

> **Cal Newport’s Digital Minimalism Standard:**  
> **"Technology is a tool to support your highest values, not a slot machine to harvest your attention."**  
> An 18-year-old who relies on parental controls to stop scrolling will drown in digital addiction at college. The emerging adult must master self-binding strategies: app time limits, grayscale display toggles, notification purges, and physical phone-docking rituals.

### 2.1 The "Front-Page Billboard Test"
Before posting any photo, video, comment, or meme online:
- *"Would I be comfortable seeing this post projected on a giant billboard in the center of my college campus, or printed on the front page of the New York Times with my name and photo next to it?"*
- If the answer is no, it does not belong on the internet. "Private" accounts, disappearing stories, and Snapchat snaps are never private; screenshots take 0.5 seconds and last forever.

## 3. Emerging Adult Cybersecurity & Financial Hygiene

1. **Hardware Two-Factor Authentication (2FA):** Enable authenticator apps (Google Authenticator, YubiKey) across all primary email, banking, and social accounts. Eliminate SMS 2FA where possible due to SIM-swapping vulnerabilities.
2. **Password Manager Architecture:** Transition from reusing the same 3 passwords to an encrypted password manager (1Password, Bitwarden) with unique, 20-character random passwords for every service.
3. **Credit Freeze Mandate:** At age 18, freeze credit files across the three major credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) to prevent identity theft and fraudulent credit cards opened in their name.

---

## 4. The Emerging Adult Family Pact (The Launchpad Agreement)

1. **The Shared Financial Contribution:** If driving a family vehicle, the emerging adult pays for their personal fuel and contributes a set monthly amount toward their car insurance.
2. **The "Safe Arrival" Communication Pact:** When driving late at night or attending parties, a quick 3-word text (*"Made it safe"*) is sent out of courtesy to housemates/parents.
3. **The Lifeline Emergency Ride Covenant:** No matter the hour (even at 3:00 AM) or location, if the teen or their designated driver has consumed alcohol or substances, parents guarantee an immediate, safe Uber ride or personal pickup with **zero questions or consequences that night**.
