## 1. AAP Clinical Policy Statement (Ages 2 to 5 Years)

The American Academy of Pediatrics sets explicit, evidence-based parameters for preschool media consumption:

> **AAP Clinical Standard (Ages 2–5):**  
> **Limit screen use to 1 hour per day of high-quality programming.**  
> Parents should **co-view media with children** to help them understand what they are seeing and apply it to the world around them.  
> **Keep bedrooms, mealtimes, and parent-child playtimes screen-free.**

### 2.1 The "Autoplay" Neuro-Trap
- In adult brains, an autoplay countdown allows cognitive evaluation ("Do I have time for another episode?").
- In a 2-to-3-year-old brain, the visual stimulus of the next episode instantly fires dopaminergic anticipation in the nucleus accumbens. When an adult steps in to shut it off mid-countdown, the toddler experiences a violent physiological violation, resulting in a rage meltdown.
- **The Mandatory Software Rule:** Disable "Autoplay Next Episode" across all streaming applications (Netflix, Disney+, YouTube) at the account level.

### 2.2 Selecting High-Signal Programming (The "Bluey & Mister Rogers" Standard)
- **High-Signal Television:**
  - Pacing matches physical human dialogue and real-world movement (>5–8 seconds per camera cut).
  - Rich socio-emotional themes: empathy, imaginative play, emotional repair, cooperation (*Bluey*, *Daniel Tiger's Neighborhood*, *Mister Rogers' Neighborhood*, *Tumble Leaf*).
  - Minimal aggressive physical comedy or rapid slapstick violence.
- **Low-Value Algorithmic Traps:**
  - Toy unboxing videos, computer-generated nursery rhyme loops with neon flashing colors, and algorithmically spawned sensory stimulation videos.

## 3. The 3-Step Predictable Media Transition Protocol

Transitioning an older toddler off a screen without tears requires concrete visual and auditory bridges:

1. **Pre-Agreed Episodic Container (Not Vague Time):**
   - Toddlers do not understand "15 minutes." Frame the boundary by completed narrative units: *"We are watching ONE episode of Daniel Tiger. When Daniel sings the goodbye song, the TV goes night-night."*
2. **The Visual Sand / Clock Timer:**
   - Place a physical mechanical visual timer (e.g., Time Timer with a disappearing red disk) directly next to the television.
   - Let the toddler turn the knob: *"When the red disk disappears, your body helps push the power button."*
3. **The Immediate Tactile Bridge:**
   - Powering off a screen creates an immediate sensory vacuum. Immediately bridge to a physical tactile activity: a cup of cold water, a plate of orange slices, or blowing soap bubbles.

## 4. Older Toddler Digital Pact Clauses (AlongSide Family Agreement)

1. **The 60-Minute Pre-Bedtime Screen Curfew:** All televisions, tablets, and phones are powered down at least 1 hour before bedtime to safeguard endogenous pineal melatonin synthesis.
2. **The Zero-Device Dinner Table:** Meals are a sacred social harbor. Neither children nor parents bring smartphones, tablets, or television into the dining room.
3. **The Device-Free Bedroom Sanctuary:** Children sleep in rooms completely devoid of televisions, interactive screens, or illuminated tablets.
